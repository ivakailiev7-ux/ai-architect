10 Scroll-Stopping Hooks for Supplement & Fitness Ads

The first 3 seconds decide whether your ad sells or gets skipped. If your ads "look nice" but don't convert, it's almost never the product — it's the hook. The hook is ~80% of an ad's performance. Swipe these. Adapt them. Test them.

01

The Direct Callout

"If you take protein every day but still feel bloated — stop scrolling."

Speaks to one person with one specific problem, and the pattern-interrupt ("stop scrolling") freezes the thumb. Generic ads talk to everyone and reach no one.

Example (gut/protein): "If your protein leaves you bloated by lunch, this 15 seconds is for you."

02

The Contrarian Myth-Bust

"Everyone's wrong about when to take their greens."

Challenging a belief creates an itch the viewer has to scratch — they watch to find out if they're the one doing it wrong.

Example (greens): "Stop taking your greens on an empty stomach. Here's what to do instead."

03

The Problem-Reveal (No-Blame)

"Your pre-workout stopped hitting — and it's not your fault."

Names a real frustration, then removes the blame. That combination makes people lean in instead of feeling attacked.

Example (pre-workout): "The reason your pre-workout feels weaker now (and the 5-second fix)."

04

The Demonstration

"No clumps. No chalk. Watch." (extreme close-up of it mixing)

For anything you consume, showing beats telling. A satisfying visual in second one buys you the next ten.

Example (protein): "Watch this dissolve in 3 seconds — zero clumps, zero blender."

05

The 3PM POV

"POV: it's 3pm, the crash is coming… except today it isn't."

Drops the viewer straight into a moment they live every day. Relatability equals retention.

Example (energy/ketones): "POV: the whole office hits the 3pm wall — except you."

06

The Social-Proof Number

"12,000 women switched to this for bloating. Here's why."

A specific number signals credibility, and "here's why" opens a curiosity loop they want closed.

Example (women's wellness): "Why 12,000 women quietly ditched their old multivitamin."

07

The 30-Day Test

"I tried 7 protein powders for 30 days. Only one didn't wreck my stomach."

Sounds like an honest review (not an ad) and opens a gap — which one? — that pulls them through.

Example (protein): use the line as-is, reveal the product at the end.

08

The Price Comparison

"$80 greens vs our $35 — same 6 ingredients. Let me prove it."

Value framing plus a "proof" promise. People stay to see the reveal / the label-to-label.

Example (greens): film the two labels side by side, circle the matching ingredients.

09

The Founder Truth

"I made this because every other sleep aid left me groggy."

An origin story delivers authenticity and the core problem in a single breath. Founders sell.

Example (sleep/recovery): talking-head or voiceover over a calm night routine.

10

The Before / After Diary

"Day 1 vs Day 30 of fixing my afternoon energy."

Transformation is the most-watched format on the internet because it promises a payoff worth waiting for.

Example (any daily supplement): diary-style cuts, same time and place each day.

The universal formula

[specific person] + [specific problem] + [pattern interrupt] — all inside the first 2 seconds. And for anything consumable: show it, don't say it.

How to actually use these

  1. Match the hook to ONE problem your product solves. Specific beats clever.
  2. Lead with it. The hook goes before the logo and the pretty shots — in the first 2 seconds.
  3. Test 3–5 hooks on the same product and let the numbers pick the winner. Never marry your first idea.
